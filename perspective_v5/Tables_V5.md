Table 1. Representative electrolyte strategies reveal multiple kinetic pathways for magnesium transfer.

| Design strategy | Representative chemistry | Mg CE (%) | Mechanistic question raised by the result | Ref. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weakly coordinating anion | Mg(CB₁₁H₁₂)₂/G4 | >94.4 | Does solvent rearrangement remain accessible as persistent anion association is reduced? | [43] S |
| Weakly coordinating anion | Mg perfluorinated pinacolatoborate (Mg-FPB) | 95 | Does weak anion association leave solvent rearrangement as the main limitation? | [47] P |

Selection: every entry reports Mg plating/stripping Coulombic efficiency (CE), the shared quantitative endpoint. First-cycle-only CE, symmetric-cell lifetimes and full-cell efficiencies are excluded from this column. Values retain the reported precision; 98.3% is a reported average. These are independent protocols, not a common-condition ranking or measurements of a desolvation barrier. The chemistry column identifies the reported design, not a complete recipe. Mechanistic questions are proposed tests, not established rate-limiting steps. P: original-paper abstract supports the CE; S: supplied AM Perspective [87], pp. 4–5 (DOI 10.1002/adma.202417652); P/S: primary CE and secondary formulation. Current, capacity, substrate, conditioning and averaging windows should be matched in future comparisons; accessed-source limits are recorded in the companion audit. Reference numbers link to DOI records. THF: tetrahydrofuran; DME: 1,2-dimethoxyethane; G2/G4: diglyme/tetraglyme; BTFE: bis(2,2,2-trifluoroethyl) ether; TMP/TEP: trimethyl/triethyl phosphate; MOPA-1: 1-methoxy-2-propylamine; MOEA: 2-methoxyethylamine; pftb: perfluorinated tert-butoxide; OTf: triflate; TFSI: bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide.

Table 2. What anion coordination changes and how to distinguish its contribution

| Possible contribution | Physical consequence | Discriminating evidence | Relevant studies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Precursor availability | Association and surrounding solvent change access to reactive states | Measure populations and transport; test transfer at matched surface conditions | [38,23] |
| Ligand departure and rearrangement | Coordination changes the route from the occupied precursor to metal | Connect a balanced pathway to exchange and Mg-specific kinetics; isolate film effects | [24,75] |
| Reduction selectivity | A coordinated donor becomes less prone to unwanted reaction | Mg partial current and gas/product balance; faster exchange is not required | [79,30] |
| Anion release or consumption | Recovery permits reuse; loss changes inventory and may activate the surface | Track active species and products versus Mg throughput; distinguish a spectator from repeated assistance | [20,80] |
| Persistent coordination | A stable intermediate exchanges or escapes slowly | Residence times and escape barriers from the occupied state; low energy alone is insufficient | [31,22] |
| Retained interphase effect | Surface products protect Mg while transfer remains possible | Check persistence after controlled liquid replacement; measure resistance and material loss | [20,86] |
| Coupled molecular and film effects | Coordination and surface evolution contribute together | Separate the initial molecular response from the retained surface contribution | [86,88] |

The rows are proposed contributions and tests, not mutually exclusive electrolyte classes. References provide relevant examples; they do not establish every mechanism listed. Recovery must accompany sustained molecular assistance to support repeated action. The outer-shell example includes a neutral additive.
